# Move the money

We build banking infrastructure, so we would love to see you handle money than handle a to-do list. This exercise asks you to build a small system where people hold a balance and can send money to each other.

It is deliberately small. The interesting part is not how much you build — it is whether the thing you build is **correct**, and whether you can tell us where your tools tried to lead you astray.

## Time

**Please spend no more than four hours on the assignment.** If you run out of time, tell us what you would have done with the fifth hour and we will assess that answer as seriously as we assess your code. An unfinished build with clear reasoning beats a rushed complete one!

## Use AI. We expect you to.

Use any AI tooling you want — Claude, Copilot, Cursor, whatever you actually work in. This is not a trap and there is no hidden preference for hand-written code. We use these tools daily and we are hiring people who use them well!

You should not need to pay for anything — free tiers, student credits or a local model are all fine. 

What we will ask you is where the tools were **wrong**. Somewhere in four hours of building a money system with an AI assistant, it will confidently hand you something that looks right and is not. We want to hear about that moment. "It worked first try" is the one answer that will not land.

## What to build

A system where accounts hold a balance and money can move between them. At minimum it should let someone:

- open an account with a starting balance
- send an amount from one account to another
- look up an account's current balance
- look up an account's transaction history

That is it. **No user interface is required** — an HTTP API, a CLI, or a well-tested library are all equally fine. No authentication, no signup flow, no deployment.

Any language, any storage (Postgres, SQLite, even in-memory), as long as you can explain the tradeoffs of what you picked.

## The rules the system must never break

These are the actual assignment. Everything above is scaffolding.

1. **An account's balance can never go negative.** Not under any sequence of requests, including ones that arrive at the same moment.
2. **A transfer is all or nothing.** There is no state of the world where money left one account and did not arrive at the other.
3. **The same transfer submitted twice is only applied once.** Networks retry. Users double-click. Assume both will happen.
4. **Amounts are exact.** If a cent goes missing anywhere, the system is wrong.

<aside>
🧪

Decide how you want to prove these hold, and then prove it. **At least one of your tests should demonstrate that rule 1 survives two transfers competing for the same money at the same time.** How you construct that test is up to you, and it is the single thing we will look at most closely.

</aside>

## What to send us

- **A Git repository.** Push it somewhere we can read it, or send us a bundle.
    
    We will read your commit history, so please work in it rather than dropping a finished project in one commit. Small commits with honest messages tell us how you think and give us better things to ask you about. We are not grading commit hygiene and we do not care about your branching strategy — we care that the history is real. If you genuinely work in one big commit, say so in the build log and tell us how you would normally do it.
    
- **A README** covering how to run it, how to run the tests, what you chose not to build, and what you would do next.
- **A build log, roughly one page.** What you set out to do, where AI genuinely helped, where it was confidently wrong and how you caught it, what it suggested that you rejected and why, and anything you shipped that you are not comfortable with. This carries real weight in our assessment — it is not a formality.

## How we will talk about it

A 60-minute conversation with your hiring manager and one other engineer:

| Roughly | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 10 min | You demo what you built |
| 10 min | Your build log, and a walk through your commits |
| 15 min | Our questions about your design decisions |
| 15 min | A small change to your own project, while we watch, with your AI tools open |
| 10 min | Your questions for us |

The live change is not a memory test. You will know the codebase — we just want to see how you work.

## What we are assessing

Whether you got real leverage out of your tools and can tell when they are wrong. Whether your instincts about correctness are good — did you test the thing that could actually break? Whether you cut the right scope to fit four hours and can say why. And whether you can explain all of it to someone who has not seen your code.

We are not assessing whether you already know how banking systems work. You do not need to, and neither did most of us when we started.
